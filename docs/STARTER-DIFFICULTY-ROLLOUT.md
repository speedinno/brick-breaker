# Easier opening rounds — rollout notes

Build 25 gives stages 1–2 ten-tile entrances, stage 3 eight, stages 4–8 six, and stages 9–12 four. Their pale silver inner tracery now breaks on impact while the outer frame stays solid, so a clearing corridor does not trap a ball indefinitely. Stage 5 presents the straight Sword before the more involved H chamber at stage 6. Stages 1–4 use a 168-unit paddle; stages 5–12 narrow through 156, 144, 132, 120, 112, 104, 96 and 88, except the stage 8 Breather widens its 120-unit base to 138. Stages 1–7 begin with one ball and a free visible shooter that tapers from 12 to 2 seconds. The stage 8 Breather and stages 9–12 start a small four-ball swarm; stages 9–12 also receive four seconds of free shooter time to open their more complex paths. Players still steer and catch later pickups. Stage 13 uses the existing advanced difficulty curve. Every eighth stage from 20 remains a Breather. Later-stage layouts do not change.

The quarter-second delayed-input controller cleared each of stages 1–4 in 20/20 seeded runs without losing a life. A tougher controller moves only once per second to the current horizontal position of a descending ball, with no trajectory prediction or pickup strategy; it also cleared all four opening stages in 20/20 seeds each, without a lost life and within two minutes. With the previous build 23 paddle widths, this slow controller cleared stage 2 only 11/20 times. Across stages 5–12, a half-second reactive controller cleared 9–10 of 10 seeds at stages 5–8, then 6–9 of 10 at stages 9–12 within four minutes. These are repeatable manual-engine proxies, not human win rates or proof of a satisfying session. Ask first-time players to attempt the opening rounds on physical phones without AUTO and record clear time, retries, aim confusion and whether they want to continue. Tune again if the first win still takes repeated frustrating attempts.

Signed-in players use a shared catalog. Existing version 3, 4 and 5 starter rows and runs remain in the database, and server completion still honors their original hashes. Build 25 uses catalog version 6 for its revised stages 1–12; stage 13 onward remains version 3. Completion history remains stored as the existing progress edition (version 3), so completion ticks and records do not split between catalog revisions. A client update during an unfinished older run may require replaying that stage because the new client requests its current catalog version; completed stages remain saved.

Before shipping build 25 on Android/iPhone/web, apply any pending `202610280016_starter_catalog_v4.sql`, `202610280017_starter_catalog_v5.sql` and `202610280018_starter_catalog_v6.sql` in that order through the established migration process. Deploy the matching API bundle. The new migration retains v3/v4/v5 rows and in-flight runs and routes new starter runs to v6. Then use two disposable signed-in accounts to fetch a revised starter, start/complete it, and confirm the completion tick, history and cross-device sync. Test already started v3, v4 and v5 runs before their expiry as well. Check a newly generated stage above 12 still uses v3.

Do not ship the client before backend migration and API deployment: its v6 starter validation will reject old shared definitions. Keep a record of the migrated schema revision, deployed API image and client commit for the release. A physical phone playtest should confirm the opening feels wide enough and stage 1 can be won without repeated frustrating retries; automated clearability alone cannot establish this.

The pasted review also identified control focus/swipe behavior, purchase retry recovery, backend test and native acceptance issues. The controller, checkout guard and stale backend test have been fixed in this branch and passed targeted software checks. Packaged Android/iPhone gameplay acceptance is tracked in [mobile build evidence](MOBILE-BUILD-3.3.md); do not claim physical-player ease from emulator/simulator checks. The public GitHub Pages demo is still the older 3.0 edition and is labelled that way in the README.
